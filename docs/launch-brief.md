# Case Room — launch brief

Prepared 17 October 2026 for an India-based PGP student with investment research experience. This is a product choice and first-user experiment, not evidence of demand or a record of a public launch.

## Shortlist and choice

| Direction | Useful first outcome | Main constraint | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decision board with an editable storyline and rehearsal tools | A team takes a recommendation through trade-offs, pitch structure, and defense | Three stages must stay connected without pretending to solve the case | **Choose:** covers all three workflows requested by the founder |
| Standalone judge-question practice bank | A team practices common objections quickly | Generic questions can miss the team’s actual recommendation and official brief | Include contextual question templates inside the chosen workflow |
| Generic case notes and evidence organizer | A team keeps research and sources together | Teams already have documents and slides; another notebook has a weak reason to switch | Include evidence cards and exports, without building a general notes app |

The founder’s research background fits the work of separating evidence from assumptions, comparing alternatives, and defending a recommendation. Access to a PGP cohort offers a practical route to observing real preparation sessions. Neither establishes that students will adopt or pay for the product.

## Small MVP boundary

The MVP focuses on one team’s recommendation, a six-slide speaking outline, pitch timing, and six contextual question cards. The critical assumption remains visible across stages. Local saving and editable backups let teams keep and transfer their work without an account.

It does not generate a designed presentation deck, verify research, grade answers, or provide shared live editing. Templates make missing information visible; they should not invent evidence. Implementation, browser checks, and hosting access must be verified separately before describing the product as a working release.

## First ten users

Recruit about ten students across three real PGP case teams, using the founder’s own cohort and club contacts. This brief authorizes no messages, invitations, or posts; none are recorded as sent.

Observe each team for 15 minutes on an actual case. Ask it to enter its brief, compare alternatives, generate and edit the storyline, and practice one judge answer. Record where members hesitate, what they keep outside the tool, and whether they export or return. Avoid using the fictional sample as proof of real usefulness.

**Hypothesis:** making the critical assumption explicit will reveal a weakness or improve an answer before the final presentation. An initial signal is at least two of the three teams identifying a concrete improvement and choosing to use the tool again on that case. Record their examples and actual return use; positive comments alone do not establish retention. Three teams remain a small convenience sample.

Ask what they would remove, when their existing workflow is faster, and whether single-device preparation or backup transfer prevents team use. Those answers determine whether the next investment is collaboration, a stronger outline workflow, or no further build.

## Draft launch copy — not posted

**Case Room: choose the move, shape the story, defend the trade-off.**

A workspace for case-competition teams preparing their recommendation, presentation storyline, and judge answers. Compare three alternatives, keep the critical assumption visible, edit a six-slide speaking outline, and practice the pitch against a timer. Take your preparation away as a case pack or editable backup.

Built for PGP/MBA teams working on one device or exchanging exports. Cases save in your browser. Questions and outlines use templates based on your inputs; your team supplies and verifies the research.

## Optional pricing experiment

Willingness to pay is unvalidated. After observing repeat use, test whether students prefer a small one-time team fee for a competition workspace or whether a campus club would sponsor access. Collect neither payments nor pricing commitments in this MVP. First establish what teams repeatedly find useful.
